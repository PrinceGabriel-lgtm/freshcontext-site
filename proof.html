What this demonstrates

A retrieval result can remain relevant while failing the boundary required for reliance.

All four scenarios can retrieve text that appears relevant. The operational question is whether that text belongs to the exact entity, version, plan, geography, time period and source role required by the task.

Semantic relevance is insufficient

A source can discuss exactly the right feature and still be wrong for the release or region in front of the user.

Uncertainty must survive

When authoritative sources conflict, the defensible output is not an invented winner. The decision should remain unresolved until the conflict is adjudicated.

Evidence changes the conversation

A reviewable record lets an operator see what was supplied, what boundary failed and why the workflow accepted, held or rejected the context.

Claim boundary

This public proof is deliberately narrower than a commercial assessment.

It does show: explicit task scope, context scope, provenance, applicability checks, preserved uncertainty and a reviewable decision record.

It does not show: customer-system access, production deployment, universal compatibility, compliance certification, autonomous remediation or a guarantee that a source is factually true.
